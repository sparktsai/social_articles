Case Study — Case B, Study 3

Looking at the same case from an ROI perspective, I see a few current conditions:

1. Customer-service investment has accumulated across the ticket system, call center, live chat, AI web chat, and the planned AI call center.

2. AI activity is visible, but activity alone does not show whether customer-service outcomes improved.

3. Several possible benefits need to be measured separately:

👉 Ticket deflection and call-volume reduction

👉 Faster response and average handling time

👉 Higher first-contact resolution

👉 Lower agent workload and cost per case

👉 Better customer satisfaction

4. A local improvement in one channel may not reduce end-to-end service cost if unresolved issues move to human agents or other systems.

5. Headcount reduction is measurable, but by itself it does not show whether service quality, customer trust, or operating capacity improved.

6. For the CEO and board, the central question is whether the new AI layer replaced cost, improved outcomes, or added another system and operating expense.

These are observations from one enterprise case, not a final ROI conclusion.
