Case Study - Case A, Study 1

AI online looks incredibly powerful.

But what does AI adoption actually look like inside real companies?

Today I’m sharing one actual enterprise snapshot — not to judge it, but to invite others to compare it with what they are really seeing inside their own organizations.

I recently documented the current AI development practices of an anonymized software company, Company A.

The company is already using AI extensively:

🟣 Engineers have moved from separate frontend/backend roles to independently owning a product.

🟣 Their responsibility now includes Frontend, Backend, Playwright E2E implementation, and Test execution.

🟣 Most implementation is generated with AI.

But the surrounding engineering environment has not changed at the same speed:

🔴 Work items remain in a separate work-management system.

🔴 Requirements live in an internal Wiki.

🔴 UI comes from Figma.

🔴 GWT scenarios live in another document system.

🔴 Engineers manually assemble these artifacts into the AI development context.

For PoCs, the company uses Vibe Coding. Once a PoC is selected for product development, the team starts again with:

Figma ➡️ rightarrow ➡️ Wiki Specification ➡️ rightarrow ➡️ GWT ➡️ rightarrow ➡️ Product Development

Other notable friction points:

🟢 AI sessions carry a significant amount of working context, with engineers creating their own skills without a unified company-wide configuration.

🟢 Testing produces Playwright reports, but code review still mainly relies on Claude Code execution results, test results, and code diffs.

🟢 Git commit and merge practices remain largely the same as before AI.

🟢 AI usage can now be tracked per employee, but those numbers are not currently connected to productivity, cost, or performance analysis.

🟢 The company’s one-year AI KPI is simple: Engineering headcount reduction.

None of this is presented as good or bad. What interests me is how uneven enterprise AI adoption can be.

Coding changes. Roles change. Testing changes. But requirements, review, traceability, governance, and measurement may move at completely different speeds.

I’m more interested in what others are actually seeing inside companies today:

🟡 Are engineers becoming broader product owners?

🟡 Is AI coding already common while review remains mostly unchanged?

🟡 Are specifications really driving development, or are teams still assembling context manually from multiple systems?

🟡 Is AI ROI being measured through productivity, cost, delivery speed, or headcount?

What does AI adoption actually look like inside your organization — not in presentations, but in daily engineering work?
