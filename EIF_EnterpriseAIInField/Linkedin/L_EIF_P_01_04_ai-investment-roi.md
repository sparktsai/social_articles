Case Study — Case A, Study 4

Looking at the same case from an ROI perspective, I see a few current conditions:

1. The most visible change so far is increased investment cost.

2. The development benefit of AI is still difficult to quantify. Possible reasons include:

👉 Scrum throughput feels faster, but customer demand absorption does not.

👉 AI speeds up implementation, but error and rework rates also increase, leaving overall delivery speed largely unchanged.

👉 Different stages from UX to QA are moving at different speeds, so unresolved bottlenecks limit end-to-end gains.

3. A future measurement model may become increasingly individual-based:

👉 AI cost per engineer

👉 Output or productivity per engineer

👉 Software quality and customer satisfaction associated with each engineer

4. From an investor perspective, current AI investment is visible, but the return is still difficult to quantify. If measurable output does not become clearer, management may face pressure to justify the investment through headcount reduction.

These are observations from one enterprise case, not a final ROI conclusion.
